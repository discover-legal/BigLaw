# IN THE UNITED STATES DISTRICT COURT FOR THE NORTHERN DISTRICT OF ILLINOIS, EASTERN DIVISION

**GREYSTONE PACKAGING CO.**, an Ohio corporation, Plaintiff,

v.

**TALBOT FRESH FOODS, INC.**, a Delaware corporation, Defendant.

Case No. 1:26-cv-09184

**COMPLAINT** — JURY TRIAL DEMANDED

Plaintiff Greystone Packaging Co. ("Greystone"), by its attorneys, for its Complaint against Defendant Talbot Fresh Foods, Inc. ("Talbot"), alleges as follows:

## NATURE OF THE ACTION

1. This is an action for breach of a written supply contract under which Talbot agreed to purchase minimum annual quantities of flexible food packaging from Greystone and then, after failing to meet its commitments, purported to terminate the contract to escape them.

## PARTIES, JURISDICTION AND VENUE

2. Greystone is an Ohio corporation with its principal place of business in Akron, Ohio.

3. Talbot is a Delaware corporation with its principal place of business in Joliet, Illinois.

4. This Court has subject-matter jurisdiction under 28 U.S.C. § 1332(a) because the parties are citizens of different States and the amount in controversy exceeds $75,000, exclusive of interest and costs.

5. Venue is proper in this District under 28 U.S.C. § 1391(b)(1) because Talbot resides in this District.

## FACTS

6. On March 1, 2023, Greystone and Talbot entered into a Packaging Supply Agreement (the "Agreement"), a true and correct copy of which is attached as Exhibit A.

7. Under Section 4 of the Agreement, Talbot agreed to purchase Products with an aggregate invoice value of not less than $2,500,000 in each Contract Year.

8. In Contract Year 2 (March 1, 2024 through February 28, 2025), Talbot purchased Products totalling $1,887,600, leaving a shortfall of $612,400.

9. Greystone invoiced Talbot for the Contract Year 2 shortfall on March 31, 2025. Talbot has refused to pay.

10. On August 15, 2025, Talbot sent Greystone a letter purporting to terminate the Agreement for cause. Talbot's purported termination was pretextual: any quality concerns had been addressed, and Talbot simply wished to move its business to a lower-cost supplier.

11. In Contract Year 3 (March 1, 2025 through February 28, 2026), Talbot purchased Products totalling $1,269,800, leaving a shortfall of $1,230,200.

12. Greystone has performed all conditions precedent to the commencement of this action, or such conditions have been waived or excused.

## COUNT I — BREACH OF CONTRACT

13. Greystone incorporates paragraphs 1 through 12.

14. The Agreement is a valid and enforceable contract. Greystone performed its obligations under it.

15. Talbot breached the Agreement by failing to purchase the minimum annual quantities and by failing to pay the shortfall amounts for Contract Years 2 and 3, totalling $1,842,600.

## COUNT II — CONSEQUENTIAL DAMAGES

16. Greystone incorporates paragraphs 1 through 15.

17. As a direct result of Talbot's wrongful termination, Greystone idled its Line 4 laminating capacity and lost profits on the business it had reserved for Talbot in the amount of $415,000.

## PRAYER FOR RELIEF

WHEREFORE Greystone requests judgment against Talbot (a) for $1,842,600 on Count I; (b) for $415,000 on Count II; (c) for pre- and post-judgment interest and costs; and (d) for such further relief as the Court deems just.
